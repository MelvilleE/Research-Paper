*** Abstract ***

- Usually, numbers under 10 are stated in words when used to describe the amount of something, e.g. '2 datasets' should be 'two datasets'. The exception to this is when you are stating an actual statistic, e.g. '1 false negative encounter' is correct. 7-day is also fine, but 'within 7-days' should be 'within seven days'. Hope that makes sense.

- What is a 'safe-heavy system'? Make sure any jargon used is sufficiently explained in the main body of the paper.

- I would avoid using 'the model's recall rate' and instead state it as 'the recall rate of the surrogate model'. Also, 'containing only the orbital features of each satellite pair' could help improve flow. 'Only a satellite pairs' orbital features' is gramatically incorrect, as you use 'a', but then refer to the plural of satellite pairs.

- I would suggest writing statistical values in 3 sig fig, unless you're absolutely sure you have 4 sf precision in your outputs.

- 'Understandably so as 1 false negative prediction could cost a satellite operator millions of dollars.' Perhaps elaborate to say, ... in the event of an unexpected collision.

*** 1 Introduction ***

- What do you mean by 'shifting the COLA timeframe'? I think this could perhaps be worded better, to specifically imply that it gives more time to assess collision risk due to lower computational requirements.

- 'This transforms the problem, then.' is awkward phrasing. 

- Instead of saying 'ref. [3]', have the Author name, followed by et al. and you can also put the year in brackets. E.g. Manfletti et al. (2025) [3].
